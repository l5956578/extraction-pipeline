# Four-way relationship map — CEFR family core

**Books in scope:**
1. **CEFR EN 2001** — foundational metalanguage & levels  
2. **Companion Volume 2020** — updated/extended can-dos & modern practice  
3. **Threshold 1990** — detailed B1-region *objective specification* (functions/notions)  
4. **Waystage 1990** — early A-region *objective specification* on the same model  

**Index:** [`README.md`](README.md) · pair maps: [`RELATIONSHIP_COMPANION_CEFR2001.md`](RELATIONSHIP_COMPANION_CEFR2001.md), [`RELATIONSHIP_THRESHOLD_WAYSTAGE.md`](RELATIONSHIP_THRESHOLD_WAYSTAGE.md)

## 1. One-picture model

```
                    POLICY / METALANGUAGE / LEVELS
                    ┌─────────────────────────────┐
                    │     CEFR EN 2001            │
                    │  (constitution of the CEFR) │
                    └─────────────┬───────────────┘
                                  │ updates & extends descriptors
                                  │ cites 2001 constantly
                    ┌─────────────▼───────────────┐
                    │  Companion Volume 2020      │
                    │  (current can-do statute)   │
                    └─────────────┬───────────────┘
                                  │ operational situations &
                                  │ function/notion density
              ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
              │                                       │
    ┌─────────▼─────────┐                 ┌───────────▼────────┐
    │  Waystage 1990    │  ──same model── │  Threshold 1990    │
    │  early objective  │     lighter →   │  freer objective   │
    │  (~A1–A2 adjacency)│     denser     │  (~B1 adjacency)   │
    └───────────────────┘                 └────────────────────┘
         SPECIFICATION INVENTORIES (exponents, themes, strategies)
```

- **2001 + Companion** = *common reference framework* (levels + can-dos + theory).  
- **Waystage + Threshold** = *pre-CEFR / parallel tradition* of **detailed communicative objectives** that **fed** the CEFR and still feed **content and situation design**.

## 2. What each book is uniquely good at

| Book | Unique strength | Weak if used alone |
|------|-----------------|--------------------|
| **2001** | Full theory: action-oriented model, how to read scales, domains/themes depth, curriculum & assessment chapters | Mediation/online/phonology/signing thin or outdated; native-speaker wording |
| **Companion** | Current can-dos; mediation; online; plurilingual scales; phonology; signing; App 7 wording deltas; modern grids | Less deep on classical task theory & full theme inventories than 2001 Ch 4–7 |
| **Threshold** | Dense function/notion/situation **exponents** for independent social English | Not levelled A1–C2; English-exemplified; not a CEFR scale set |
| **Waystage** | Early-stage selection of the same model; honest lighter load | Same limits; smaller inventory |

## 3. How they illuminate each other

### 3.1 Waystage ↔ Threshold
See pair doc. **Same components, different density and freedom.** Path metaphor is authorial (station → threshold).

### 3.2 Threshold/Waystage → CEFR 2001
- Functional/notional work **preceded and informed** CEFR’s action-oriented and communicative categories.  
- CEFR **levels** (A1–C2) provide a **common scale** that Threshold/Waystage never were.  
- CEFR **domains** (public/personal/occupational/educational) generalise situation thinking that Threshold spells out in extended characterisation.  
- CEFR **strategies** (Ch 4–5 scales) formalise what Threshold/Waystage list as compensation / discourse structuring.

**Prefer Threshold/Waystage when:** building concrete **exponent lists**, **transaction scripts**, **theme vocabulary** for English adult objectives.  
**Prefer 2001 when:** placing those objectives on a **common multi-language level scale** and theory of competences/activities.

### 3.3 Threshold/Waystage → Companion 2020
- Companion **can-dos** state levelled performance; Threshold/Waystage state **objective content**.  
- Companion **App 5** domain examples for online/mediation are modern *situations*; Threshold situations are richer for **classic travel/social English**.  
- Companion **mediation/online** have no Threshold/Waystage equivalent — do not force-fit.  
- App 7 wording changes affect CEFR scales, **not** Threshold exponent lists (different artifact type).

**Prefer Companion when:** reporting level, self-assessment, mediation, online, phonology, signing.  
**Prefer Threshold/Waystage when:** “What language exponents for hotel/restaurant/socialising at early vs freer stage?”

### 3.4 Companion ↔ 2001
Unchanged: see `RELATIONSHIP_COMPANION_CEFR2001.md`. Companion is statute of can-dos; 2001 is constitution + deep scheme.

## 5. Mapping adjacency (levels)

| CEFR level | Companion | 2001 | Waystage | Threshold |
|------------|-----------|------|----------|-----------|
| Pre-A1 | Scales + App 2 | Limited | Partial spirit | — |
| A1–A2 | Full can-dos | Full can-dos | **Objective density** | Prerequisite to Threshold path |
| B1 | Full can-dos | Full can-dos | Below | **Objective density** |
| B2–C2 | Full can-dos | Full can-dos | — | Beyond (not the book’s aim) |

“Objective density” ≠ “is the CEFR level.” It means: **most useful historical specification inventory for that region.**

`threshold_situation → cefr_domain`  
`companion_scale_id → cefr_2001_section`  
`companion_scale_id → app7_change_row` when wording changed

## 7. Anti-patterns (family)

1. Treating Threshold/Waystage as CEFR descriptor documents.  
2. Ignoring them entirely once Companion exists (lose exponent/situation wealth).  
3. Using only Companion App 5 for “all situations.”  
4. OCR-only phonology from App A.  
5. Collapsing four books into one “CEFR PDF” answer.  
6. Claiming EVP/EGP coverage from these four.

---

## 8. Student-facing short form

> **CEFR 2001** tells us how Europe thinks about language learning and levels.  
> **Companion 2020** gives the **current can-do scales** (including mediation and online).  
> **Waystage** and **Threshold** spell out, for English, **what a basic vs freer social objective contains** — situations, functions, notions, strategies — and they still power good course design under those levels.
